# Draft — to Heather Nelson

Send it however reaches her: Facebook, email, or through her attorney. If
she has a lawyer handling the estate, sending it to both is better — it
means she doesn't have to forward anything while grieving.

**Do not attach the works list to the first message.** Offer it. Let her
ask. A spreadsheet of her husband's songs arriving unrequested is a heavy
thing to open.

---

Heather,

This is Greg Putnam. Erik and I wrote together for about twenty years.

I'm not writing to ask you for anything, and there's nothing here you need
to answer quickly. I wanted you to know some things about Erik's music that
are yours now, because nobody would blame you for not knowing they exist.

Erik is a credited writer on 87 songs in my catalogue. His share across
them adds up to the equivalent of about 40 complete songs, owned outright.
Every one is registered with an ISRC. These aren't sentimental credits —
they're an asset, and they pay out for a long time.

I've already asked ASCAP to do two things:

  - direct Erik's writer royalties to you as his successor
  - end the administration arrangement that was sending Ershkinsongs'
    publisher share to an outside company, so it comes back to his side

Neither of those changes my share. I'm not seeking a penny that Erik and I
didn't agree to. Our agreements were always in writing and always fair, and
I intend to honour them exactly as they stand.

ASCAP will probably contact you to confirm you're his successor. That's
routine. If it's easier, I can send whatever documentation your attorney
needs directly to them.

There's one more thing. I believe I hold the largest collection of Erik's
own original recordings anywhere — well over a hundred. Most were never
titled or registered. They're his, and right now they earn nothing because
nobody knows they exist. I'd like to identify and register them properly so
they can pay your family. That's work, not money, and I'm glad to do it.
Only if you want it done.

Whenever you're ready — and only then — tell me what would actually help.
If it's none of this, that's a complete answer.

I'm still knocked sideways by losing him. I heard him every day for twenty
years and I still do, because the work is right here. I know that's nothing
next to what you're carrying.

He was the best musician I ever worked with. I wouldn't have a catalogue
without him.

Greg

Gregory D. Putnam
G Putnam Music, LLC
gputnam@gputnammusic.com

## Notes for me, not for her

**What I deliberately did not put in this letter**

- No dollar figure. I don't have income yet and a number she remembers is
  a promise I might not keep.
- No mention of a memorial page. That's hers to approve or refuse, and
  raising it in the same breath as money makes the money feel conditional.
- No request. Not one. If she reads it and does nothing, it still did its
  job.

**What I can safely commit to once she answers**

A percentage of actual revenue from Elements built on songs Erik co-wrote —
never a fixed sum. A percentage of zero is zero. Nobody is owed, nobody is
disappointed, and it grows by itself if the KUTs work.

**Before any memorial page exists**

Her written consent to use Erik's name, likeness and story. Those are hers
now. No page until she says yes.
